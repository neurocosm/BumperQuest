* The Twist: The flippers don't just flip straight up. They can change angle, slide along the bottom rail, or morph in size depending on the current "mode" of the board.

## 2. Dynamic Geometry (The Wireframe Morph)

* The game board starts as a simple circle or box. As the ball hits specific bumpers, the perimeter wireframe shifts shapes—morphing from a flat layout to a Tempest-style starburst or a Qix grid.
* Changing the shape alters the gravity vector lines, forcing the ball into entirely new physics loops.

## 3. Ghost/Hunter Entities

* Just like Pac-Man, autonomous vector "enemies" wander the wireframe lanes.
* They don't destroy the ball; instead, they act as moving bumpers. If a ball hits a "Ghost," the ghost is temporarily scattered into loose vector fragments, and the ball receives a massive speed boost.

SPIN-VORTEX is an ambient, self-playing arcade simulation designed as a mobile-first Progressive Web App (PWA). ------------------------------
## 🏛️ Core Architecture & Concept
The game runs an autonomous, closed-loop physics simulation on a native HTML5 <canvas> element. No direct button inputs are required for core gameplay. Instead, the simulation plays itself perfectly while the user uses physical phone manipulation to tilt and twist the board state, reminiscent of nostalgic, old-school Cracker Jack handheld pocket mazes.
------------------------------
## 🕹️ System Features & Technical Manifest## 1. The Quad-Flipper Matrix (Double-Pinball)

* Description: Four autonomous, AI-driven pinball flippers are permanently anchored in the four corners of the screen (Top-Left, Top-Right, Bottom-Left, Bottom-Right).
* Behavior Logic: A continuous trajectory tracking script calculates the ball’s vector line. When the ball enters a corner striking zone, the corresponding flipper triggers with perfect mathematical precision, launching the ball back into the central chaos loop.
* Visual Style: Sharp, bright geometric vector wedges that flash white upon mechanical activation.

## 🎛️ 2. The Central Turntable Bumper

* Description: A classic analog vinyl record acts as the massive gravitational and mechanical focal point at the exact center of the canvas field.
* Ambient Loop: Spins continuously at a baseline 33 RPM or 45 RPM speed, emitting a low-register vinyl surface crackle via the Web Audio API.
* The Scratch-FX Trigger: The moment the pinball collides with the turntable rim:
* The background audio track immediately shifts, firing an energetic, raw vinyl scratch sound effect.
   * The vinyl record graphic jumps frame-by-frame, briefly reversing or slipping its rotation pattern.
   * Global physics parameters (ball velocity multipliers, vector illumination colorways) randomize instantly, altering the board's behavior.

## 🕷️ 3. The Tempest Spider (The Core Guardian)

* Description: Inspired by the crawling threats of Tempest, an autonomous spider-like line-art entity patrols the perimeter rings immediately surrounding the central turntable.
* Behavior Logic: Acts as a defensive shield. Using an intercept algorithm, it attempts to align itself directly between the oncoming pinball and the record center to "protect" the turntable from being struck.
* Deflection: If the ball collides with the spider, it functions as a highly elastic bumper, launching the ball away at an accelerated velocity toward the outer lanes or corner flippers.

## ⚡ 4. Electric Fence Barriers

* Description: High-voltage vector grid lines that slice through the mid-lanes of the board, serving as unstable temporary structural blockades.
* Behavior Logic: These fences periodically crackle into existence, flickering on and off based on the audio track's rhythm.
* Impact: When the ball hits an active electric fence, it doesn't drain. Instead, it gets violently repelled with a randomized erratic trajectory modifier while generating an 8-bit buzz sound sample.

## 🔺 5. Variable Moving Ghosts (Geometric Hazards)
Floating, pure vector outline shapes roam the playfield lanes. They lack complex textures, relying entirely on geometric minimalism and distinct movement profiles:

* 🔵 The Circle: Sweeps smoothly in wide, perfect orbital paths around the turntable, creating a predictable deflection zone.
* 🟩 The Square: Moves via jagged step-logic, snapping across a localized virtual grid pattern at random intervals.
* 🔺 The Triangle: Darts aggressively in strict, sharp-angled zig-zags across the screen, mimicking high-velocity screensaver paths.
* 🛑 The Rectangle: Acts as a heavy, slow-moving pendulum shield that slides lazily side-to-side, screening lanes leading to the flippers.
